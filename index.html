Cookie Cats: Does Moving the First Gate Affect Retention?

A/B test analysis · 90,189 players

Analyzed an A/B test from the mobile puzzle game Cookie Cats that moved the first progress gate from level 30 to level 40, to see whether it changed how many players came back.

Finding: 7-day retention was significantly higher with the gate at level 30 (19.84% vs. 19.03%, p = 0.003), with no significant difference at day 1. The gap was largest among players who played 40–99 rounds, the group most likely to have reached a gate.

  • Validated the data first: no duplicate players, excluded 3,994 players who never played and one impossible outlier.
  • Used chi-square tests and a breakdown by rounds played to locate where the effect comes from.
  • Built an interactive dashboard and wrote up limitations, such as rounds only approximating levels reached.

Student Perceptions of Grading

Undergraduate researcher · University teaching & learning center · Poster presented at a 2026 research symposium

A mixed-methods study of how grading practices shape undergraduates' learning experiences. I led the quantitative analysis of a pre-survey measuring students' beliefs about grading, fixed mindset, and "brilliance" (whether academic success requires innate talent), which complemented the team's qualitative focus-group coding.

  • Visualized Likert-scale responses and checked each construct's reliability with Cronbach's alpha.
  • Ran a factor analysis to identify which grading-belief items best captured the underlying construct.
  • Compared constructs across demographic groups (STEM vs. non-STEM, first-generation status, income, gender).

Takeaways: students largely rejected fixed-mindset views of intelligence, yet many still believed some people have an innate gift for academics. Many agreed grades reflect learning, while doubting that the highest grades mean the most learning.

World Building Project

Tile-based exploration game · Java

Every seed generates a new world of rooms and hallways. Explore with limited line of sight, collect every flower to unlock the exit, and escape.

  • Procedural generation: random non-overlapping rooms, each joined to its nearest neighbor by a hallway so every room is reachable. The same seed always rebuilds the same world.
  • Line of sight: tiles are drawn only if they're in range and the straight line to them isn't blocked by a wall.
  • Compact saves: since the seed recreates the world, a save stores just the seed, the player's position, and which flowers were collected.
  • Tested by having a bot play 2,000 generated worlds to confirm every one is winnable and that saving and loading mid-game never changes the outcome.

Pacman Search Agents

Artificial intelligence course project · Python

Implemented search algorithms that let Pacman plan paths through mazes, then designed heuristics for harder problems.

  • Depth-first, breadth-first, uniform-cost, and A* graph search.
  • Modeled a "visit all four corners" problem and wrote a heuristic to guide A* through it.
